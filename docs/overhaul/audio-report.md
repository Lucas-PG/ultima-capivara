# Audio pass report (branch audio-pass)

The whole game's audio was rebuilt. Every sound is now generated by the project's code and baked in a worker, played through an explicit mix with a limiter, distance and occlusion models, zoned ambience and a small score. The two complaints were proven, traced to their cause and fixed, with tests that fail on the old design.

## 1. The always-on white noise

**Proof.** `tools/audio/capture-mix.mjs` records the real master output in Chrome (practice match, standing still, Chrome muted) by routing every connection to the destination through a recorder, and it can mute and solo each looping source. `tools/audio/analyze-capture.ts` measures the octave-band floor (10th percentile of 46 ms frames), its movement (10th to 90th percentile) and loudness.

| Old engine, idle, default sliders | Broadband floor 250 Hz to 8 kHz | Movement in 1 to 8 kHz | Loudness |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Full mix | -54.7 dBFS, flat across 6 octaves | 1 to 4 dB | -42.6 LUFS |
| Every looping source muted | none (digital silence between events) | | -61.7 LUFS |
| Loop 2 alone (insects) | steady -61 to -64 dBFS from 4 to 16 kHz | 1 to 2 dB | -41.9 LUFS |
| Loop 1 alone (surf) | steady -63 to -65 dBFS at 0.5 to 1 kHz | 3 to 4 dB | -49.6 LUFS |

**Cause.** `startAmbience()` looped three buffers of white noise, two seconds long, forever: "insects" was white noise band-passed at 3.6 kHz, "surf" white noise low-passed at 950 Hz and "wind" brown noise at 460 Hz, at fixed gains that were never modulated. The hiss was the idle mix: it sat 12 dB under the mix's own loudness, and the 2 s loop repeated audibly.

**Fix.** Those loops are gone. Beds are designed and breathe: surf in wave cycles (build, crash, receding fizz) panned toward the nearest open sea, gusty wind that grows with height, leaf flurries that fall to silence, harbour lapping, the Cachoeira waterfall, all long (10 to 16 s) seamless loops at low sample rates. Island life comes as positioned one-shots from the world (birds from real trees, gulls on the coast, town, farm, chapel, mangrove). Under a roof the beds drop 10 dB and lose their highs; they duck about 7 dB under combat and recede at low health.

**After (Chrome, same capture):** broadband floor -67.4 dBFS, 29 to 45 dB of movement in every octave. The offline test renders the idle mix at five places (beach, town, hill, forest, harbour) at default sliders and requires no more than one steady octave above -66 dBFS and a floor under -56 dBFS; a replica of the old beds fails the same test.

## 2. Gunfire that sounded like footsteps

**Proof.** Analysis of the old bundled samples (ffmpeg decode plus `tests/audio/metrics.ts`): the gunshot recordings measured -24.7 to -27.8 LUFS momentary, while the footsteps measured -19 to -27.5, so the steps were louder in the files. The shots decayed 20 dB in 10 to 25 ms (shorter than the steps' 50 to 115 ms), with spectral centroids of 650 to 1500 Hz against the steps' 300 to 590 Hz, all in 24 kHz files with nothing above 12 kHz. In the mix a remote sprinting step and a remote shot at the same distance landed within a few dB of each other, and both went through the same dark distance filter: at range, two dull low thuds.

**Cause.** Same loudness, same band, same short envelope, same distance treatment.

**Fix.** Gunfire and steps are now different on every axis, and a test holds each one:

| | Guns (near) | Guns (far) | Paw steps |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Loudness at the mix | own shot 18+ dB over own steps; remote shot 10+ dB over a sprinting step at the same distance, 2 to 30 m | crossfades in from 18 to 70 m, -4 dB per doubling | -6 dB per doubling, fades out by 24 m (walk), 32 m (sprint), 8 m (crouch) |
| Energy below 200 Hz | 0.30 to 0.62 | 0.39 to 0.60 | 0.00 to 0.13 |
| Decay to -20 dB / -40 dB | 80 to 610 ms / 270 to 1290 ms | 735 to 1395 ms / to 2405 ms | 65 to 115 ms / 85 to 195 ms |

Guns: crack or pop, sub blast, low body, a mid "bark" per gun, the mechanism (slide, bolt chatter, the M4's buffer-spring sproing, heavy DMR clack, revolver cylinder ring, shotgun pump and sniper bolt timed to the viewmodel's cycle) and an outdoor tail with slapback echoes. Far versions trade the crack for a dark pop and a rolling terrain echo; rifles keep a faint supersonic snap. Steps: a capybara pad (kept above 150 Hz, because a low thud is what distant gunfire sounds like) and a second softer paw, plus the ground's answer: grass rustle, dirt grit, a dark sand hush, stone nail ticks, a hollow board, a metal ring, a shallow-water slosh. Material comes from what the paws touch: collider tops (wood, stone, metal), shallows (`waterAt`), then the painted terrain (`terrainSurface`).

## 3. Everything else that was rebuilt

- **Mix** (`src/sound/mix.ts`): every buffer normalized to -20 LUFS, one loudness table for the hierarchy (own and enemy gunfire, confirms, handling, enemy steps, own steps, ambience, music), a 6 dB output trim, a limiter (-8 dBFS, 20:1, 1 ms) and a soft clipper that is linear to 0.7 and never reaches full scale. Other players' gunfire has its own bus that your hit, headshot and kill confirms duck 6 dB for 250 ms. Combat ducks ambience and music.
- **Spatialisation and occlusion:** HRTF on High (equal-power on Low), direction only, with distance handled by the models; speed-of-sound delay past 20 m; walls between you and a shot (collider-grid ray) take 5 dB and most of the highs off gunfire, 8 dB and more off steps. Near misses crack past your head at the closest point of the bullet's path.
- **Weapons and handling:** all eight weapons plus the facão swing and the coconut launcher, coconut blasts near and far, bullet impacts per surface (with the odd stone ricochet), every viewmodel reload cue (mag, slide, bolt, pump, shell, cylinder, eject, speedloader, coconut), remote reloads from where the enemy stands, spectated reloads as a fallback.
- **Movement:** material steps and landings weighted by fall speed, jump, crouch cloth, sprint gear, swim strokes, entry and exit splashes, mud soaking, trampolines.
- **Feedback:** body hit tick, headshot bell, kill chime, armour-break shatter, directional damage thump audible on small speakers, storm bite, low-health heartbeat, pickups per kind (weapon, ammo, armour, helmet, items) with rarity chimes, chests, consumables, supply drop call, landing and opening, respawn, ladder upgrades rising in pitch.
- **Match flow:** countdown ticks, a samba whistle at the start, zone-closing horn, storm bed outside the zone and a growing rumble near its wall with crackles and thunder, the plane cabin, the drop plane heard from the ground, freefall wind by speed, parachute opening and canopy flutter.
- **Music** (original, composed in code): a bossa nova menu loop (nylon guitar, bass, vibraphone, shaker, rim), a light batucada on the drop, a sparse endgame pulse, victory and defeat stingers, and the dance emote's samba from the dancer. Other emotes get capybara whistles and purrs.
- **UI:** menu hover, click and back are soft mallet notes through the engine; the UI no longer opens a second AudioContext.

## 4. Measurements

- **Bake:** about 2.3 s in the worker in Chrome, 297 buffers, no main-thread work; combat sounds bake first. Budget: 9.1 M samples (36 MB) on High, 5.3 M (21 MB) on Low with mono beds and music and fewer variants. The worker bundle is 44 kB; the 69 kB of old MP3s are gone.
- **CPU:** `sound.update()` per frame during a full-auto hunt (Chrome, `?timing=1`, three runs on a heavily loaded machine): p50 0.1 ms, p95 0.2 to 0.3 ms, p99 0.4 to 0.9 ms, rare spikes up to 4 ms. HRTF panners are pooled and pre-built at unlock (creating them was the earlier 5 to 14 ms spikes).
- **Loudness in Chrome at default sliders:** full-auto hunt -19 LUFS integrated, 0.02% of samples in the clipper's knee, peak -2 dBFS; idle ambience -40 to -52 LUFS by place (offline). The old game ran -29 LUFS in combat and -43 idle, mostly hiss.
- **Voices:** own gunfire 12 (Low 10), remote gunfire 20 (12), steps 10 (6), effects 24 (16), island life 8 (5).

## 5. Tests

`npx tsc --noEmit` clean; `npx vitest run`: 101 files, 853 tests pass.
- `tests/audio-bank.test.ts` (15) renders the real bank and measures it: every sound finite and normalized, loops seamless, memory budget, gunfire against footsteps on loudness, envelope and low-band energy, every gun pair distinct, far versions darker and longer, step materials distinct and short, idle ambience with no steady broadband floor (and the old beds failing it), still audible and breathing, beds dropping under a roof, the clipper transparent below 0.7 and bounded, a worst-case pile-up staying below full scale.
- `tests/audio.test.ts` (36) runs the real engine on a fake Web Audio context (`tests/audio/fake-context.ts`): the output graph ends limiter then clipper, every id the engine asks for exists, voice caps, own shots dry and remote shots spatial and delayed with the far layer, occlusion, near misses, ducking, confirms, reloads, pickups, storm, music states, countdown and stingers, emotes, ambience, and all the earlier movement, swimming and mud intents.

## 6. Tools

- `tools/audio/lab.html` (open on a dev server): audition every sound and readability scenarios at the real mix levels (enemy SMG at 30 m then steps at 8 m, the same pistol from 5 to 240 m, idle beach, forest, town, harbour, menu music).
- `tools/audio/render-bank.ts`: bake time, memory, per-sound metrics, WAVs and spectrograms.
- `tools/audio/capture-mix.mjs` and `analyze-capture.ts`: master-output capture in Chrome (idle with mute and solo per loop, combat hunt with audio cost, battle royale drop).
- `docs/overhaul/audio-research.md`: how CoD, VALORANT, Apex, Overwatch and Battlefield design for readability, with sources.

## 7. Outside the audio files

One-line hooks, all necessary: `src/ui/ui.ts` sends menu sounds to a new `uiSound` callback instead of its own AudioContext; `src/main.ts` wires that callback and adds `__capivara.audio()` to the dev diagnostics; `src/settings.ts` raises the music default from 0.25 to 0.5 now that there is a score. `public/audio/*.mp3` removed; `docs/audio-sources.md` and `docs/assets.md` updated.

## 8. Known issues

- Nobody has listened yet: every judgement here is from measurements and spectrograms. Please audition `tools/audio/lab.html` and a match before merging; the procedural guns may want a round of taste tuning (the layers and levels are all in `src/sound/weapons.ts` and `mix.ts`).
- No per-enemy threat score (Overwatch and Apex style); occlusion, distance and voice pools cover the common cases.
- Remote gunfire does not change with the shooter's facing (VALORANT's toward, side and away perspectives).
- The waterfall is by nature a steady broadband source, but only near Cachoeira (fades out by 70 m).
- The Tucano supply plane has no flyby sound (only the delivery call); the island has no doors and no time of day, so neither is voiced.
- Players with saved settings keep their old music slider (0.25), so the score is quieter for them until they raise it.
- Memory is 36 MB of AudioBuffers on High; baking lazily per mode could halve it if needed.
