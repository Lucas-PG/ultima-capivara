ULTIMA CAPIVARA: THREE NEW WEAPONS
Research and concept specification, 29 September 2026

ASSUMPTIONS AND SUCCESS CRITERIA
The nine weapons in the request are the authoritative roster. The slingshot is removed, even though its old reference PNG remains. These are concepts, not implemented or playtested balance changes. Ranges, capacities and timings below are starting targets. The three additions must produce different combat decisions, recognizable silhouettes and memorable first-person handling without replacing existing roles.

The supplied shotgun, SMG and coconut-launcher sheets define the requested finish: polished stylized 3D, softly beveled forms, painted tropical motifs, dark steel, brass, hardwood and restrained wear. They take precedence for these sheets over the older docs/art-direction.md prohibition on PBR metal and scratches. That older guidance is left unchanged. No additional weapon variants are proposed.

RESEARCH: WHAT THE SIX GAMES OFFER
These are representative examples across releases, not claims about today's competitive meta. Mechanical descriptions come from the linked publisher sources. Statements about why those mechanics are fun are design interpretations.

Call of Duty: the broad rifle, SMG, shotgun, LMG, marksman and sniper families let players choose handling and engagement distance. The SWAT 5.56 is a signature three-round burst marksman example. Its appeal is a compact, decisive firing phrase followed by a readable recovery window. Heavy LMG handling makes sustained fire a commitment. Transfer the contrasting rhythms and reload personalities, not another general-purpose automatic rifle.
Sources: Black Ops 6 weapon guide: https://www.callofduty.com/guides/blackops6/pre-game/call-of-duty-guides-black-ops-6-multiplayer-pre-game-weapons-and-loadouts; SWAT 5.56 in the beta guide: https://www.callofduty.com/guides/blackops6/getting-started/call-of-duty-guides-black-ops-6-multiplayer-beta-everything-you-need-to-know.

Battlefield: support LMGs, exemplified by the KE7 in Battlefield V, give the squad sustained pressure, while SMGs and shotguns own closer encounters. Battlefield 1's balance explanation explicitly preserves stronger ranged LMG performance and slower close-range acquisition. The fun is holding a crossing while teammates move, with an obvious vulnerability when caught unprepared. Transfer capacity and handling tradeoffs; avoid forced camera shake or aim penalties inflicted on opponents.
Sources: Battlefield V weapon roster: https://www.ea.com/en-gb/games/battlefield/news/weapons-vehicles-gadgets-reinforcements-list-battlefield-5; Battlefield 1 balance rationale: https://www.ea.com/games/battlefield/news/improving-battlefield-1-weapon-balance.

Valorant: the Sheriff, Judge, Guardian, Operator and Odin embody precision sidearm, automatic shotgun, deliberate rifle, sniper and heavy machine-gun identities. Strong differences in cadence and commitment make recognition meaningful. The Odin's spectacle and the Guardian's deliberate aim illustrate how satisfying sustained tracking and careful shots can coexist. Ultima Capivara already covers most of the precision and close-range space; a heavy support identity is the larger omission.
Source: Valorant official arsenal: https://playvalorant.com/en-us/arsenal/.

Apex Legends: the Bocek adds draw-and-release timing, projectile anticipation and a different physical handling fantasy. Its launch notes explain that drawing increases shot power and projectile speed. The same notes group the G7, 30-30, Triple Take and Bocek as marksman weapons, demonstrating how distinct delivery can differentiate similar ranges. Transfer anticipation and leading a moving target without restoring the removed slingshot.
Source: Apex Legacy update and Bocek: https://www.ea.com/ea-play/news/legacy-update-patch-notes.

Overwatch: Zarya's short-range beam and Roadhog's Scrap Gun plus Chain Hook make tracking and positioning central to weapon identity. Roadhog's hook is a hero ability, not a standalone firearm. The transferable lesson is a readable positional interaction, rather than copying a full hero kit or a guaranteed hook-and-kill combo.
Sources: Zarya: https://overwatch.blizzard.com/en-gb/heroes/zarya/; Roadhog: https://overwatch.blizzard.com/en-us/heroes/roadhog/; Hook repositioning balance rationale: https://overwatch.blizzard.com/en-gb/news/patch-notes/live/2023/01/.

Fortnite: Midas' Drum Gun, Kit's Shockwave Launcher and the Rocket Ram illustrate a spectrum from recognizable firearm to memorable utility object. The Last Resort announcement presents weapons and movement tools together. The appeal is a clear physical gag with tactical consequences: rhythm, propulsion or repositioning. Transfer an object players understand at a glance, not a second explosive lobber competing with Lanca-coco.

GAPS IN THE CURRENT ARSENAL
Pistola and Trinta-e-oito cover backup fire and punchy precision. SMG Canarinho and Doze cover close tracking and close burst damage. M4 covers flexible mid-range automatic fire. Carabina and Sniper cover deliberate medium-long and long-range precision. Lanca-coco supplies arcing explosives, and Facao supplies melee.

No major distance band is empty. The missing experiences are sustained support with a costly reload, committed multi-shot bursts with recovery between them, and a nonexplosive precision projectile that changes positioning. A second shotgun, another bolt-action rifle or another splash launcher would mostly duplicate existing decisions. The three proposals below address duration, firing rhythm and interaction respectively.

1. MARACATU
Real-world class: drum-fed light machine gun, fictional 5.56 mm platform.
Role and range: support and lane holding at roughly 15-45 m. Helps teammates cross exposed ground and contests groups through ammunition endurance.
Fire mode: full automatic, approximately 540 rounds per minute, immediate firing without spin-up. Stable planted fire; strong movement spread and slow aim acquisition. No artificial suppression debuff.
Magazine and reload: 60-round detachable drum, approximately 4.0-second tactical reload or 4.6 seconds empty. Replace the entire drum beneath the receiver; cycle the charging handle only when empty. No belt or top-feed-cover sequence.
What makes it fun: a deep, steady percussion-like report and sustained multi-target tracking. The last rounds produce a subtle mechanical rattle that makes the coming reload legible. Its long exposed reload creates a natural handoff to teammates. Per-bullet damage and handling must keep the M4 the better mobile dueling choice; no escalating damage for holding the trigger.
Brazilian identity and silhouette: Pernambuco maracatu percussion inspires a large drum magazine with painted wooden side plates and brass tension-lug shapes. Deep indigo receiver, coral and cream sunburst stencils, warm hardwood stock and foregrip, dark steel barrel shroud. A small crown-shaped stencil evokes the procession visually without pretending to reproduce a specific group's insignia. No literal musical instrument bolted onto a rifle. Broad drum and thick vented barrel distinguish it from the M4.
First-person reload: right paw maintains the grip while the left releases and supports the heavy drum, lowers it, seats a fresh drum with an upward shove, and checks its latch. Empty reload ends with one broad charging-handle pull. Weight is expressed in a short muzzle dip, not a long camera wobble.
First-person inspect: tilt left to expose the sunburst drum, tap its wooden plate twice with a paw, then rotate to show the brass lugs and return to ready. Cosmetic percussion only; cancel immediately on fire input.

2. FREVO
Real-world class: burst-fire assault rifle, compact fictional 5.56 mm bullpup.
Role and range: mobile precision peeking at roughly 20-50 m, between the M4's continuous tracking and Carabina's single-shot cadence. Low open sights rather than the Carabina's 3x optic.
Fire mode: exactly three rounds per trigger press, approximately 900 rounds per minute within a burst and a 0.28-second recovery after its last shot. No full-auto mode. A started burst completes unless interrupted by a weapon-state action; a fresh press begins the next burst after recovery.
Magazine and reload: 24 rounds, eight complete bursts; detachable box magazine behind the grip. About 2.3 seconds tactical or 2.8 seconds empty. A partial magazine fires only its remaining rounds, never free ammunition.
What makes it fun: the audible three-beat phrase rewards keeping all three shots on a moving capybara, then stepping into cover during recovery. Predictable climb within each burst makes practice useful. Tune to prevent a full-health body-shot elimination in one burst. Missing part of the burst is meaningfully costly; the M4 retains forgiving continuous fire and Carabina retains scoped single-shot precision.
Brazilian identity and silhouette: Recife and Olinda frevo umbrellas inspire broad segmented wedges in coral, turquoise, golden yellow and cobalt, painted onto the receiver. A small umbrella stencil on the stock, brass controls, oiled hardwood cheekpiece and grip connect it to the existing arsenal. Keep the barrel and muzzle visibly ahead of the forward grip. Short bullpup body and rear magazine must read differently from Canarinho's wire stock and the M4's conventional layout. No loose ribbons across the sights.
First-person reload: cant the weapon inward, reach behind the firing paw to remove the magazine, insert a fresh one with a firm heel-of-paw tap, then thumb the oversized bolt release on an empty reload. Keep the magazine path visible below the sightline rather than hiding it entirely offscreen.
First-person inspect: roll the colorful flank into view and drum three fingertips lightly along its painted wedges. Briefly show the opposite side and the rear magazine before returning to aim. The sound echoes the firing cadence without resembling a live burst.

3. ARPÃO DA MARÉ
Real-world class: pneumatic fishing speargun / harpoon gun, with deliberately fictional game tether behavior.
Role and range: skill-shot disruption and team setup at roughly 8-25 m; projectile hits remain possible to 35 m, but tether interaction ends at 25 m. No explosive splash and no long-range sniper replacement.
Fire mode: hold primary fire to charge for up to 0.65 seconds, then release one visible, gravity-affected harpoon. A tap fires a slower, weaker shot without tether utility. Full charge enables a brief tether on a direct enemy hit. Secondary fire reels once during that window; it is not another projectile mode.
Magazine and reload: one loaded harpoon, no detachable magazine; a reserve bundle supplies replacements. Approximately 2.5 seconds to fit a fresh shaft and reset the line cassette. The onboard pressure system resets during reload. Reel-back animation retrieves the line connector, not a free reusable damaging projectile. A live tether must end before reloading.
What makes it fun: anticipation, leading the target, a satisfying cable snap and a precise tug that exposes someone at a corner for a teammate. Reel moves the victim at most 1.5 m toward the shooter over 0.25 seconds, with no stun or aim lock. The target can still shoot. A 0.7-second visible tether window, broken line of sight, range limits and solid collision all provide counterplay. Do not pull through cover or chain-disable: grant 2 seconds of pull immunity after a tug. Charge movement is slower and a miss leaves a long single-shot recovery. Direct damage must remain below a full-health kill, including headshots. No grappling traversal, persistent traps or environmental one-shot bonus is bundled into the concept.
Brazilian identity and silhouette: coastal jangada fishing materials, turquoise-painted wood, cream wave stencils, red-orange fishing-float accents, salt-darkened steel, aged brass bands and a short sisal-wrapped grip. A slim spear rail, one protruding harpoon, compact pressure cylinder and enclosed circular line reel beneath the forebody make it unmistakable. Its narrow rail and reel differ from Lanca-coco's flared bamboo muzzle and coconut hopper. This is a fantasy above-water adaptation, not a real speargun engineering proposal.
First-person reload: release the spent line cassette, thumb a fresh compact cassette into the reel housing, slide a replacement shaft onto the top rail, then close the chunky loading lever with a short pressure hiss. Keep the sharp end pointing away from the camera; paws never sweep across it. Charge is communicated by a rising mechanical whine and a moving brass indicator tab, with no numeric gauge required.
First-person inspect: tilt to show the wave-painted wood and orange float, spin the reel's outer inspection ring a quarter-turn, watch the short slack loop settle, then return to ready. Do not launch a spear during inspect.

SHARED ART AND ANIMATION REQUIREMENTS
Exactly three views per sheet: dominant orthographic left side above, muzzle pointing left; smaller opposite right side below left, muzzle pointing right; rear three-quarter view from behind the weapon's left side below right. Show identical geometry, ammunition feed, motifs and colors across all three views. Warm light-grey seamless studio backdrop, soft grounded shadows, no text, labels, hands, characters, scenery, UI or extra loose props.

The written first-person animations assume capybara paws with three broad fingers and one opposable thumb. Manipulation surfaces are oversized for those paws. All inspect animations are cosmetic and immediately cancellable. No hands appear on the design sheets.

PROTOTYPE ACCEPTANCE CHECKS
Compare Maracatu and M4 across mobile duels, sustained lane defense and reload punish windows. Compare Frevo with M4 and Carabina using peek timing, burst misses and moving targets. Test Arpão da Maré against corners, collision, simultaneous tethers, range breaks and consecutive pulls, verifying that victims retain firing control and never cross solid geometry. These are future gameplay checks, not tests claimed to have run for this art task.

VISUAL REVIEW AND CORRECTIONS, 29 SEPTEMBER 2026
This section supersedes conflicting mechanism interpretations above. Sources for first-person animation principles and evidence limits are in animation-research.md. All added timings remain proposals, not observed game timings or implemented behavior.

Maracatu: the ribbed wood beneath the barrel is a FIXED handguard bolted at both ends, not a pump. The sunburst drum is a rigid detachable ammunition housing with wood veneer over a metal shell, not a flexible percussion head. Existing side sheet shows unlike receiver controls: establish left-side ejection opening and opposite-side linear charging handle; do not mirror both openings onto both sides. Keep the release above the drum accessible when the support paw leaves the fixed foregrip. Drum removal goes down along its feed neck, with no top-cover/belt gesture. The support thumb lies muzzleward on the rear of the wood, fingers down-under; drum remains behind this contact and cannot intersect the wrist. Hip uses a lower-right mass, centered iron aim with rear aperture and front post, both below the target lane. Sprint uses two-paw low carry because of mass. Inspect taps a stationary drum plate only, never unlatches it. Tactical 4.0 s: release 0.45, clear drum 1.00, retrieve 1.80, align 2.55, seat/check 3.20, ready 4.00. Empty 4.6 s adds charging pull at 3.65, return/regrip by 4.60. Hand operating a far-side control must be visible as a reach, not an invented near-side control.

Frevo: retain rear magazine behind the firing grip. Keep a full magazine extraction corridor downward behind the firing wrist; roll the butt outward for reload and move the left paw behind the right. Do not turn it into a conventional front-magazine rifle. Add a clearly defined RIGHT-side ejection opening above the rear magazine, behind the forward firing grip and below the cheekpiece, with rear deflector. The left flank is solid except its existing brass charging/control plate. The original opposite-side sheet omitted this necessary port; corrected sheet is the design reference. Keep the bolt-release paddle distinct from the magazine latch and reachable on the rear left above the well. Use a low open notch and front post, as the rear three-quarter sheet indicates. Existing side silhouettes are not proof of aperture geometry. Hip support is on rear wood foregrip in FRONT of firing grip, clear of muzzle. Sprint remains compact two-hand carry. Tactical 2.3 s: cant 0.20, remove rear mag 0.55, present 1.05, seat 1.55, recover 2.30. Empty 2.8 s adds rear-left release contact 2.10, then support recovery by 2.80. Inspect 2.1 s: left flank 0.50, opposite flank 1.30, return 2.10. No camera jump or mag passing through forearm.

Arpao da Mare: original sheet has no defined left-paw purchase and runs tether under the pressure cylinder where a support paw would close. Add a FIXED wood saddle around the rear half of the cylinder, immediately ahead of the reel. This is a protective foregrip, not a pump. Route the line through an enclosed guide on the RIGHT far side from reel to nose, outside the entire grip envelope. No loose line crosses either paw or trigger guard. Keep the reel/cassette housing, orange float and grip mutually clear; float attachment sits below and forward of the trigger guard, with a short loop that cannot swing through it. Pressure reset is deliberately fictional, not a real mechanical design. The corrected sheet defines this contact arrangement.

The Arpao FP board's top-right is a FOCUSED READY hold along the spear rail, not conventional secondary-fire ADS. Secondary remains the previously specified reel action; no unrequested ADS mechanic or zoom is added. Right grips wrapped handle with index at trigger; left holds fixed saddle, never the reel, bare spear or line. Hip keeps spear point projecting into scene and clear of center. Sprint keeps two-paw low carry and minimal float swing. Reload 2.5 s: end tether before animation, spent cassette clear 0.35, new cassette seat 0.80, replacement shaft aligned away from player 1.25, shaft fully seated 1.85, loading lever closed 2.15, ready 2.50. No free duplicated shaft or hand across tip. Inspect 2.1 s: lift 0.35, show wave/float/reel 1.15, small outer-ring check without releasing cassette, return 2.10. Exactly one loaded shaft, no detachable ammunition magazine.

The first-person images are concept studies subject to the README checklist. Any failed paw pose or inconsistent control in those images does not supersede these explicit corrections. Capacities, damage, tether rules and balance remain untested proposals. Slingshot stays excluded.
